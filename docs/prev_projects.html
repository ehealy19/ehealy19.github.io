Economics 304: Final Project

Description: Salaries and Sports: A Look into the Financial Effect of College Athletics

This econometrics papers investigates the financial impact of college athletics on post-graduation salaries. Using a person-year dataset of college students from four institutions between 2012 and 2019, the data includes labor, personal statistics, job salaries, and sports participation information. The research examines the effect of varsity versus club-level sports, as well as gender differences in salary outcomes. Healy employs log-log regression models and incorporates fixed effects to account for individual, time, and sport-specific variations, correcting for heteroscedasticity and autocorrelation. The results indicate a significant negative impact of varsity sports participation on salaries, with varsity athletes earning less than non-athletes on average, while female varsity athletes tend to earn more compared to their male counterparts, although the latter result is less precise. The findings highlight the complexity of measuring the financial returns of college sports and suggest that athletics may not benefit post-college earnings as previously assumed.

Economics 384: Research Project

Description: The Impact of Act 39 on Employment, Hours, and Earnings in the Alcohol Retail Market in Pennsylvania

This project examines how Pennsylvania’s Act 39, which removed Sunday restrictions on alcohol sales, influenced labor market outcomes within the state’s beer, wine, and liquor retail industry. Using quarterly data from the Pennsylvania Department of Labor’s QCEW dataset, this study employs a difference-in-difference approach to assess changes in employment, wages, and hours worked across 67 counties. By comparing the beer, wine, and liquor industry with other non-retail sectors, the analysis aims to isolate the effects of Act 39. The project also explores potential spillover effects in border counties and runs robustness tests, including a placebo test with neighboring states, to validate the findings. Through these methods, the study provides insights into the labor market impacts of deregulation in a state-controlled alcohol market.

Economics 396: Senior Undergraduate Thesis

Description: This research examines the influence of directorial quality on both box-office revenue and critical acclaim within the U.S. entertainment industry. Data was collected from multiple public sources, including Kaggle, The Numbers, and IMDb, covering films released from 2002 to 2016. The dataset includes key variables such as domestic gross, production budget, IMDb ratings, director experience, and accumulated awards. Data cleaning involved merging datasets, addressing missing values, and ensuring consistency across fields like production budget and genre classifications. Methodologically, the analysis applied Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression to estimate the effects of directorial quality on film success. Two primary independent variables were constructed: one based on the director’s historical box-office revenue and the other on critical acclaim measured through awards and nominations. The models incorporated controls for film characteristics (e.g., runtime, MPAA rating) and director-specific factors (e.g., previous acting experience, gender). Robustness checks were performed to validate the results, and additional fixed effects were added to account for the film’s production budget and previous director-actor collaborations. This approach allowed for a comprehensive analysis of how leadership in film production impacts both financial and critical outcomes.

Math 210: Optimization Project

Description: Optimization of Bus Routes Utilizing MATLAB

This optimization project works to develop a model for efficiently allocating buses across 20 stops and 8 routes. The project aimed to minimize passenger wait times and bus operation costs by determining the optimal number of buses per route. The model used binary matrices to identify route connections, calculated rider distribution proportions, and optimized the allocation using iterative approaches. The final solution balanced service quality and operational expenses, providing a data-driven strategy for efficient bus routing.

Math 210 (Section 2): Final Project

Description: Minimum Spanning Trees: An Investigation of their Applications and Algorithms

This project explores the implementation of Prim’s algorithm to find minimum spanning trees (MSTs) from an undirected, edge-weighted graph. The coding aspect involves creating a graph representation with a 2D array and iteratively selecting the minimum weight edges to build an MST. The project also applies these concepts to a real-world example using Haverford College’s campus map, calculating paths between buildings to optimize for minimal total distance while connecting all points. Additionally, the project compares the MST approach with maximum flow solutions, highlighting the efficiency and difference in outcome between these algorithms. Math 382: Research Project

Description: Cutting the Cheese: Simulating Infectious Disease Spread with Fiction

This paper simulates the spread of infectious diseases using a fictional model inspired by the “cheese touch” game from Diary of a Wimpy Kid. The model is based on a modified Susceptible-Infected-Recovered (SIR) framework, incorporating unique rules from the game, such as passing the infection through touch, a time-limited infection period, and a defense mechanism represented by crossing fingers. Multiple iterations of the model are developed, each with varying assumptions about infection spread, and the simulations are conducted using MATLAB. Key metrics, such as the basic reproduction number (R₀), are calculated to assess the spread dynamics across different iterations. Comp Sci 106:

Description: Interpretation of Wordle

This project recreates the New York Times Wordle game using Java, with a focus on implementing core data structures and efficient algorithms. The main coding components include a Wordle class to manage game logic, handling word selection, guess validation, and providing feedback on guesses using loops and conditionals. A CSVReader class reads from a file of potential words, while arrays are used to store data efficiently. The program uses ANSI color codes to simulate the game’s feedback visually in the command line. The Random and Scanner classes manage word selection and user input, respectively.
